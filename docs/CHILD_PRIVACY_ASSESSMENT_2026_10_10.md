# RecallStride child privacy and legitimate interests assessment

**Scope and date:** 10 October 2026; the personal A-Level study service, including students aged 16–17, accounts, notes, revision recommendations, written practice, local coding and optional usage measurement. This is an implementation-grounded impact and legitimate-interests assessment, not a claim of regulatory certification. It must be updated when actual processing changes.

## Purpose, people and data flow

RecallStride provides a private study workspace and independent OCR H446 revision support. Signup requires an email, authentication and a 16-or-over declaration. It does not independently verify age. A-Level content makes users under 18 foreseeable, so the same high-privacy defaults apply to all accounts, including accounts created before the age declaration was introduced.

Personal study records include profile preferences, notes, practice responses, confidence, attempts and review dates. They are saved in the Cloudflare-hosted account database so students can return to their work and see progress. The adaptive recommendation uses those records to order suggested practice; the student remains free to choose a different activity. It does not determine an exam grade, school placement or another significant external decision.

Coding execution, source, virtual files, example inputs and planning notes stay in the browser. Account coding records contain stated local check outcomes and assistance counters, not source code. Optional usage events are separately consented, off by default and limited to defined events/IDs. They are still account-linked personal data, not anonymous data. No advertising profiles, precise location tracking, camera/microphone access, third-party marketing trackers or AI-provider processing are part of this service.

Cloudflare provides hosting, persistent SQLite storage, recovery and incoming email routing. Resend supplies transactional verification, password-reset, subscription and support-notification email through the RecallStride domain; Google Gmail holds the support inbox. Stripe supplies payment collection and billing. The operator can access data for a support, security or operational purpose. Existing shared notes/archived class records follow their membership permissions; personal notes do not become public simply because an account exists.

## Legitimate interests assessment

**Purpose:** provide the educational account and tools the learner requests, protect account access, respond to enquiries and resolve operational failures. Core free/student account operations rely on legitimate interests, including where a child’s contractual capacity should not be assumed. Adult subscription processing uses the payment agreement; optional business measurement uses consent; necessary tax records use applicable legal obligations.

**Necessity:** email/authentication identify the saved account and allow recovery; private notes and study evidence enable returning to the learner’s work; bounded security records prevent abuse and diagnose failures. A local-only guest route is available for coding, but cannot replace a saved cross-device revision account. The app does not need passports, full birth dates, precise location, contact books, advertising profiles or health records. Those are not collected for ordinary use.

**Balance:** a learner can reasonably expect the app to save work and show practice suggestions after choosing those tools. The useful educational result supports the learner rather than an unrelated advertiser. Risks still include unwanted disclosure, excessive retention, inaccurate suggestions and pressure to purchase. Controls include private accounts, authenticated membership checks, optional analytics off, no marketing profiling, clear limitations, free coding access, neutral topic choice, export, session revocation, erasure requests, short telemetry retention and human support. Users may object to legitimate-interest processing; support considers the request and offers export/closure where a core saved-account function cannot operate without the minimum records.

The educational justification for study suggestions is confined to the requested study service. It does not authorise advertising, compulsory use, grade predictions or sharing performance with a school. Adding any of those purposes requires a new assessment and appropriate user choices.

## Impact assessment and controls

| Risk to the learner | Severity and likelihood before controls | Current mitigation | Residual assessment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Other people access personal notes or study records | High severity; possible | Authentication, session/device controls, private-workspace membership checks, password-reset session revocation, support identity checks | Reduced likelihood; account theft remains possible and must be monitored |
| Shared-device browser reveals source or answers | Moderate severity; possible | Account-scoped drafts, coding sign-out cleanup, clear browser-storage notice, export/clear controls, advice to sign out and clear site data | Residual risk if a shared browser is left unlocked; browser storage is not represented as encrypted or remotely erased |
| Feature events collected without meaningful choice | Moderate severity; possible | Opt-in off for all users; both browser and server respect it; withdrawal removes stored optional account events; no text/source in event contents | Reduced likelihood; new events must keep the allowlist and consent boundary |
| Data kept after it is useful or after account closure | Moderate severity; possible | Implemented daily/startup expiry, support/audit/telemetry limits, cascade deletion, restricted recovery and reapplication of erasure requests, manual inbox disposal | Operational monitoring needed for inbox and backup copies; legal holds are specific and reviewed |
| Study suggestion interpreted as official grade or mastery | Moderate severity; possible | Practice/evidence context and published limitations; no official grade guarantee; students choose topics; local coding passes do not certify all inputs | Reduced likelihood; review actual student feedback and misleading copy |
| A young learner unknowingly creates a recurring charge | Financial harm; possible | 16+ account declaration; adult/guardian permission at paid purchase; clear monthly price/renewal; accessible cancellation; full 14-day refund on every charge | Declaration is not verified age or parental approval; refund/support handles mistakes and disputed purchases |
| Parent, payer or teacher receives data without authority | High severity; possible | No automatic personal-account disclosure to parent/school; support checks authority and the learner’s rights; existing shared membership only | Reduced likelihood; support must apply the identity procedure each time |
| Provider/network incident or overseas processing | High severity; possible | HTTPS, established provider terms, limited categories, published provider/transfer notice, restricted administrative access, recovery procedures and incident response | Provider and operator security remain continuing obligations; monitor incidents and review material provider changes |

No biometric age verification is used. A proportionate age declaration avoids collecting more sensitive identity documents for a limited educational tool. Because age declaration can be inaccurate, it is not used to relax privacy settings for anyone. Discovery of an under-16 account is handled through support, appropriate export and account closure rather than covert age inference.

No public feed or direct-message product is offered in the student experience. Existing collaboration requires authorised membership and must not be treated as parental surveillance. New classroom monitoring, wider sharing, public profiles or messaging requires a fresh risk review before implementation.

## Consultation, oversight and review

The operator has reported student testing and navigation feedback. No separate privacy interviews, expert legal review or regulator consultation are represented as completed. The published short privacy explanation, data controls and support route allow questions and objections; record those themes and use them to improve explanations and safeguards. Consult affected students and parents where appropriate when changing data uses, rather than infer privacy acceptance from a successful UI test.

Review this assessment after an incident, a material provider or retention change, an expansion to younger students, public/classroom sharing, marketing profiling, externally significant automated decisions, or evidence that the existing controls do not address a learner’s actual risk. Residual risks above require continuing operation of the controls. If a proposed change leaves a high risk unmitigated, seek the consultation required by the applicable law before that processing.

The assessment uses actual product boundaries and does not claim universal children’s-code compliance from a checkbox or a document alone.
